Qatar Weather Month by Month

What every month of the year actually feels like in Qatar — and how to plan around the heat, humidity, UV and dust.

Guide · Updated June 2026 · ~6 min read

Qatar has a hot desert climate (Köppen classification BWh), but reducing the whole year to "hot and dry" misses how dramatically conditions shift between January and August. The difference between a pleasant 22 °C December morning and a 44 °C, 70%-humidity August afternoon is the difference between an ideal outdoor season and genuinely hazardous heat. This guide walks through the year so you can plan trips, exercise, events and outdoor work around what each month really delivers.

The Two-Season Reality

Although you'll sometimes see Qatar's year split into four seasons, in practice residents experience it as two: a long, intense summer roughly from late May through September, and a mild, comfortable cool season from November through March. April and October are the short transitional shoulders between them — and they are, for most people, the best weeks of the year to be outdoors.

The Cool Season (November–March)

This is when Qatar is at its most liveable. Daytime highs sit in the low-to-mid 20s through December and January, dropping to genuinely cool evenings that occasionally dip below 12 °C inland. It is the season for the Corniche, desert camping near Sealine, dhow trips and outdoor dining. The two caveats are fog and rain: radiation fog can form on still winter nights and reduce morning visibility on the roads, and the rare but intense winter downpours — February is statistically the wettest month — can cause flash flooding in Doha because of the flat terrain and limited drainage.

The Shoulder Months (April & October)

April and October are short windows that reward early planning. Mornings are warm rather than hot, humidity is low, and UV — while rising in April — is still manageable before midday. October in particular feels like relief: after a brutal summer, the first week the high drops below 37 °C is noticeable across the whole city. The trade-off in April and May is dust: this is peak Shamal season, when northwesterly winds can lift desert sand and cut visibility sharply.

The Summer (June–September)

Qatar's summer is genuinely extreme and deserves to be treated with caution rather than bravado. It is not just the air temperature — regularly 43–44 °C — but the combination with Gulf humidity that makes it dangerous. In July and August, a 43 °C reading at 65% humidity produces a "feels like" heat index near 56 °C, which overwhelms the body's ability to cool itself through sweating. This is why Qatar enforces a legal midday outdoor-work ban from 11:30 to 15:00 between 1 June and 15 September. For everyone else, the practical rule is simple: schedule anything physical for early morning, stay hydrated, and treat the middle of the day as indoor time.

Planning tip: If you're choosing when to visit Qatar or schedule an outdoor event, the window from mid-November to mid-March is comfortable nearly every day. December has the most reliable pleasant weather of the entire year.
