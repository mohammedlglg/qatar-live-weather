🌍 Understanding Qatar's Climate

Original editorial content — updated seasonally

🌡️ Qatar's Climate Overview

Qatar has a hot desert climate (Köppen BWh), characterised by extremely hot, humid summers and mild, dry winters. The country sits at approximately 25°N latitude on the Arabian Peninsula, surrounded by the Arabian Gulf on three sides, which moderates temperatures somewhat while adding high humidity during the warmer months.

Annual rainfall averages only 70–80 mm, almost all falling between November and March. Snow is essentially unknown.

🔥 Heat Safety in Qatar

During June–September, outdoor conditions can be dangerous. The combination of temperatures above 40°C and humidity above 60% creates a heat index that far exceeds the thermometer reading.

Key safety thresholds:

— Feels like >46°C: Avoid outdoor activity
— UV Index >8: Full sun protection required
— Visibility <1 km: Dust/sandstorm conditions
— Humidity >75%: High heat stroke risk

Qatar mandates a midday work ban (11:30–15:00) for outdoor workers from 1 June to 15 September each year.

🌬️ Wind & Dust Events

The Shamal — a northwesterly wind — is the most significant wind pattern in Qatar. It can bring dust and sandstorms, especially in spring. Sustained Shamal winds of 40–60 km/h combined with low humidity cause rapid desiccation and reduce visibility to under 1 km.

The Kaus (southeasterly) and Barih (northerly) are secondary wind patterns. During summer nights, the sea breeze from the Arabian Gulf provides some relief to coastal areas like Doha and The Pearl.

☔ Rainfall & Humidity

Rain in Qatar is infrequent but can be intense when it occurs. Winter rainfall events can cause flash flooding in Doha due to limited drainage infrastructure. The wettest month is typically February (average ~18 mm).

Humidity is highest from July to August when sea surface temperatures peak. Coastal Doha regularly records relative humidity above 80% at night during these months, while inland areas like Dukhan remain drier.

🌅 UV Index & Sun Hours

Qatar receives 9–11 hours of sunshine per day year-round, making it one of the sunniest countries globally. The UV index peaks between April and September, regularly reaching 10–12 (Very High to Extreme).

UV safety guide:
— UV 1–2: No protection needed
— UV 3–5: Sunscreen SPF 30+ recommended
— UV 6–7: High — hat and shade essential
— UV 8–10: Very High — avoid midday sun
— UV 11+: Extreme — stay indoors 10:00–16:00
